 ABSTRACT: Decreasing the level of use of Pt in proton exchange membrane fuel cells is of
 great research interest both academically and industrially. In this work, we systematically
 studied the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) following the four-electron association
 mechanism at various Pt−Bi surfaces with density functional theory calculations. The results

 To further explain the change in the PDS in ORR, we
calculated the d-band centers (εd) of the Pt species on the
different PtxBi1−x surfaces. For the sake of accuracy, in this
work, the εd of Pt for each surface model was calculated by
using the HSE06 functional44 and a 2 × 2 × 1 k-point mesh.
Figure 5a shows that with an increase in Bi content, the d-band
of Pt shows an obvious contraction for narrowing and a
significant downward shift of the d-band center position, which
may be attributed to the fact that the tensile strain caused by Bi
effectively changes the d-electron structure of Pt. In addition,
we also calculated the relationship between the d-band center
of Pt and the OH adsorption energy on the PtxBi1−x surfaces
(Figure 5b). The results show that there is a linear relationship
between the εd of Pt and the OH adsorption energy in the Pt−
Bi bimetallic systems, which also illustrates that the lower the
d-band center, the weaker the interaction between OH and the
Pt−Bi bimetallic surfaces, which is consistent with the
previously reported experimental results.30,45,46 Therefore,
GOH* can be effectively regulated by adjusting the d-band
center of Pt in the bimetallic systems, and the d-band center
can be then used as another good descriptor of ORR activity.
Therefore, PtBi(100) with the lowest d-band center gives the
weakest OH* adsorption energy and is closest to the vertex of
the volcano diagram (Figure 4b).
 However, it is noteworthy that the OH* adsorption on the
Pt(111) surface does not conform to the d-band center model.
This is mainly because the valence electron of Pt is 5d96s1 and
the outermost d orbitals are almost fully occupied, so as the
outermost orbitals of OH*, causing strong repulsive inter-
actions between the outermost electrons of Pt and OH* and
leading to a weak OH* adsorption, which is consistent with
previous reports.47
